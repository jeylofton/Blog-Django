Who is Jey Lofton?

My name is Jey Lofton. I’m a filmmaker, writer, producer, entrepreneur, Army veteran, and software engineering student. A lot of who I am comes from the experiences I’ve had, the things I’ve survived, and the decision I made a long time ago to keep growing instead of allowing my past to define where I end up.

I spent over 15 years in the U.S. Army and eventually became a Staff Sergeant. I deployed multiple times, served in leadership positions, and spent years teaching and training others. The military gave me discipline, structure, adaptability, and a different perspective on life. It also taught me that plans don’t always go the way you expect, so you have to know how to adjust without losing sight of the mission.

Today, a big part of my focus is filmmaking. I’m the founder of Invidious Entertainment LLC, where I write, produce, and direct stories that deal with real human experiences—love, trust, survival, relationships, accountability, family, trauma, personal growth, and the consequences of the choices we make.

I’m not interested in making stories just for entertainment. I want my work to make people think, question their perspective, and hopefully see situations differently than they did before. That’s why the philosophy behind Invidious Entertainment is, “Challenging your perspective and inspiring change.”

Some of the projects I’ve developed include Bottom Boy Survivor, Anticipation, and Social. Even though they’re different stories, they all come back to something I care about: people dealing with the reality of their decisions and figuring out who they are when life forces them to confront themselves.

I’m also studying software engineering because I believe in continuing to evolve. I don’t like putting myself in one box. If there’s something I want to understand or build, I’m willing to learn it. For me, technology is another form of creating something from nothing, just like filmmaking.

I think a lot about ownership as well. Ownership of my work, my ideas, my businesses, my finances, my decisions, and ultimately my time. I’m working toward building assets and creating something that lasts beyond me, especially for my family.

As a person, I’m very direct. I value loyalty, accountability, consistency, respect, and honesty. I pay attention to actions more than words. One thing I live by is, “Once a man paints you a picture, don’t let him paint you another one.” To me, that means when someone shows you who they are through their actions, you should pay attention instead of constantly making excuses for them.

At the same time, I believe accountability and empathy can exist together. I can understand why someone became who they are and still believe they have a responsibility for what they do next. That applies to other people, but it also applies to me. I believe becoming a better version of yourself requires being willing to look at yourself honestly.

At this stage of my life, I’m focused on creating, learning, building, providing for my family, and becoming more intentional about how I spend my time. I’ve lived a life centered around service and responsibility. Now I’m building a life centered around ownership, purpose, creativity, and leaving something meaningful behind.
